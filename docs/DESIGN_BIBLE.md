## 0. Research notes and sources

Research was done through web search. Direct page fetches were blocked by the sandbox egress
policy, so facts were cross-checked between several search-result summaries:
Wikipedia, Dunderpedia (The Office wiki), TV Tropes recap and character pages, IMDb (episode and
"Threat Level Midnight: The Movie" 2011/2019), Nerds and Beyond / Consequence (2019 full-cut
release), Cinemablend "10 things from the full cut", Looper, Paste, Give Me My Remote, OfficeQuotes.

### Three layers, kept separate

| Layer | What happens there | How the game uses it |
|---|---|---|
| **1. The fictional movie** (Michael's film) | Scarn, Goldenface, the bomb in the puck, the Scarn dance, Cherokee Jack's ghost, the satellite | The game's playable story (Ch. 1–11) |
| **2. Michael's filming process** | Shot over ~11 years; cast changes, people aging between scenes; office spaces as sets; Michael fighting with Dwight over whether Samuel is a robot; the "most expensive scene" being a hostage's head exploding, replayed several times; Michael insisting it was "integral to the story" | The movie-inside-the-game looks homemade: recycled sets, office props, cardboard, late reactions, boom mics, continuity errors |
| **3. The Office episode** (S7E17, Feb 2011) | Michael screens the finished movie for the Dunder Mifflin staff (most of them are in it). They laugh at the wrong moments; Holly is less excited than he wants; Michael is hurt, then comes round to people enjoying it | Optional **Screening Mode** intermissions: stylized office silhouettes watching, camera glances, awkward applause |

### Verified story beats (movie)
1. Scarn is "the best secret agent in the business". Newspaper clippings (narrated) say he saved the
   NFL, "MBA" (sic) and NBA All-Star games; his wife **Catherine Zeta-Scarn** was murdered by
   **Goldenface**; he retired to become "a mild-mannered paper salesman".
2. His robot butler **Samuel L. Chang** wakes him (hungover in the film; in our PG version, asleep
   in his chair) — **President Jackson** wants to see him about Goldenface.
3. There is a bomb at the **NHL All-Star Game**; Goldenface has taken the **concession-stand
   workers hostage**. Goldenface's reasoning: blow up the All-Star games so the trophies explode
   and everyone becomes gold like him.
4. The game is three days away and Scarn can't play hockey, so he trains under **Cherokee Jack**
   and becomes "one of the hottest hockey players in the country".
5. **Tryout**: the All-Star Game takes one talented amateur each year. (In the film, Scarn kills the
   other player to get in. We turn this into non-violent slapstick credential swapping.)
6. Samuel intercepts the name **Jasmine Windsong**, a singer at **The Funky Cat**, secretly working
   for Goldenface. She sings gibberish that, **recorded on a tape recorder and played backwards**,
   says the hostages are **under the stadium**. A Goldenface assassin gets her with a blow dart
   (we show a sleep dart and a theatrical faint).
7. Goldenface shoots a hostage (a stand-in for Toby) to show he means business; the moment is
   replayed several times. (We keep the gag but not the gore: Goldenface's golden pistol, Toby
   falls over, four replay angles, and Michael's megaphone insisting it is "integral to the story".)
8. Scarn confronts Goldenface, **is shot**, recuperates, and learns **the President is in league
   with Goldenface**.
9. At **Billy's** bar (Billy is played by Andy), someone plays G9 on the jukebox: **"The Scarn"**,
   the song Scarn used to dance to with his wife. The whole bar dances and his confidence returns.
10. At the All-Star Game, **Cherokee Jack's spirit** tells Scarn to take his frustrations out on the
    puck. Scarn hits the puck out of the stadium into space; it hits a **satellite** (restoring
    reception on Billy's bar TV), then comes down on **Goldenface** and explodes.
11. Scarn returns to **Scarn Manor** with the trophy. Samuel is revealed to be a robot
    (Michael gave in to Dwight's demands).

Jack; Jan → Jasmine Windsong; Andy → Billy; Stanley → narrator; Toby → the hostage; Karen, Pam and

### Who is playing whom (and how they talk)

The film's joke is that everyone is still themselves under the costume. Every line in the game
is written through two filters: the movie character, and the coworker Michael cast.

| Role | Played by | How the lines are written | Voice casting (synthetic, tuned) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Michael Scarn | Michael Scott | Mangled idioms, big words used slightly wrong, puns explained after landing, needs the room to love him, "that's what she said" at the worst moment | Mid-high and fast: blend of two Kokoro male voices, +3 semitones, presence boost (median F0 about 136 Hz) |
| Samuel L. Chang | Dwight Schrute | "Question:", "False.", beets, bears, Renaissance fairs, keeps almost admitting he is a robot | Clipped, nasal EQ, slightly lower than Michael (about 127 Hz) |
| Goldenface | Jim Halpert | Deadpan, reads villain lines like a cue card, asks if that was enough "ha"s, breaks to blame Michael's script | Relaxed and dry, small room instead of villain reverb (about 119 Hz) |
| President Jackson | Darryl Philbin | Chill, calls everyone "man", evil but unbothered, owns the stadium | Deep and smooth (about 92 Hz) |
| Cherokee Jack | Creed Bratton | Non sequiturs about the IRS, warrants, past lives and a van | Old and gravelly, low-passed (about 123 Hz) |
| Jasmine Windsong | Jan Levinson | Icy, vain, candles, falls for pathetic men | Cool, low female (about 183 Hz) |
| Billy | Andy Bernard | Cornell, a cappella, "Nard Dog", relentlessly upbeat | Bright and bouncy (about 143 Hz) |
| Narrator | Stanley Hudson | Reads the script flatly and occasionally comments on it | Very deep and slow (about 94 Hz) |
| Hostages | Pam, Kevin, Toby | Pam is kind, Kevin is about the chili, Toby is Toby | Soft (Pam, about 200 Hz), very low and slow (Kevin, about 84 Hz), muffled (Toby) |
| Nurse | Helene (Pam's mom) | The movie's "make sure everything is working properly" gag | Mature female (about 194 Hz) |

Voices: blends of Kokoro-82M's stock voices, pitch-shifted and EQ'd toward each actor's register
and energy. No recording of any actor is used, sampled or cloned.

## 5. Comedy and Tone Bible

**Rule zero:** the game is well made; the movie inside it is not. Controls, readability and UI are
never the joke.

Why the source is funny, and how each thing becomes gameplay:

| Source humour | Game translation |
|---|---|
| Michael takes absurd things completely seriously | Scarn's lines are sincere. Characters never say "this movie is bad" |
| Office spaces as grand locations | Every background is dressed office furniture (see §4) |
| Recycled locations and shots | Driving montages reuse three backdrops; the "Scranton establishing shot" appears three times with different captions |
| Cheap effects | Explosions are clip-art with a visible white border; fire is flat orange paper; snow is visibly the same 12 flakes |
| Characters waiting for cues | Goons fall 0.4 s after being hit; hostages cheer late; one extra looks at the camera |
| Dramatic freeze frames and slow motion | Freeze frame with a title stamp at every chapter end; slow-mo "Nooo" on key beats |
| Replaying the "expensive shot" | Toby's (non-graphic) shooting plays four times from new angles while Michael yells through a megaphone |
| Overconfident one-liners | Scarn quips after fights; a **Dramatic Pose** button (F) exists purely for flair and is tracked as a stat |
| Continuity errors across years of filming | Samuel's glasses change between shots; a calendar changes years |
| The screening (episode) | Screening Mode: silhouettes, a camera glance, polite applause |

Office references (light and affectionate, never mean): the World's Best Boss mug (as "World's Best
Secret Agent"), beets, the Dundies, Pretzel Day, Kevin's chili, the George Foreman grill injury,
Serenity by Jan candles, WUPHF, the Sabre printer fire, Jim's stapler in Jell-O, "that's what she
said" (once, gated as a hidden achievement), Prison Mike, "I declare…", Parkour, the Fun Run, the
Scranton Strangler (headline only), Schrute Farms, "Bears. Beets. Battlestar Galactica."
**Content line:** no slurs, no ethnic humour, no punching down. Samuel's name is used as in the
film, with no caricature. Violence is cartoon-style (goons fall over, nobody bleeds).

## 7. Game Design Research → decisions

- **Floor-plane "belt" view** (Streets of Rage / Scott Pilgrim / NES *Ice Hockey*): one camera
  language for shooting, stealth, exploration and hockey, so a single cut-out rig style works everywhere.
- **Input responsiveness**: fixed accel/decel curves (no floaty ice except in hockey), 100 ms input
  buffering for dodge/shoot, dodge i-frames (Celeste/Enter the Gungeon-style forgiveness).
- **Juice** (Vlambeer's "Art of Screenshake"): muzzle kick, 60–80 ms hitstop on knockdowns, camera
  kick opposite to recoil, big bright bullets, knockback, dust particles, sound on every action.
  All screen shake is scaled by the Reduced Shake setting, and flashes are removed by Reduced Flashing.
- **Rhythm** (Rhythm Heaven, Rhythm Quest devlogs): notes scheduled on the audio clock; windows
  PERFECT ±55 ms, GOOD ±110 ms, OK ±160 ms; user latency offset; generous Assist mode.
- **Stealth** (Mark of the Ninja): vision cones always visible, deterministic patrols, readable "?"
  → "!" suspicion ramp (0.6 s grace), instant checkpoint retry per room.
- **Checkpoints** at every beat (usually 1–3 minutes apart); "Restart checkpoint" in pause.
- **Difficulty curve**: tutorial ≤3 min; enemies telegraph (wind-up pose + flash); boss patterns
  are learnable; the Assist toggle halves damage and widens timing windows.
- **Onboarding**: one verb at a time, contextual key hints that swap for gamepad/touch.
- **Accessibility**: subtitles/captions for all voice and important SFX, visual alternative for the
  audio puzzle, high-contrast indicators, reduced flashing/shake, volume sliders, pause anywhere.

## 8. Technical Plan

- **Stack** (versions checked on npm, Sep 2026): React 19.3, TypeScript 6.0 (typescript-eslint
  supports up to 6.0.x; TS 7 native is not yet supported by the linter), Vite 8, **Phaser 4.2.1**
  (Phaser 3 reached its final release, 3.90, in May 2025; Phaser 4 is the current stable line and is
  API-compatible for the features used here. Its bundled v3→v4 migration guide was followed: no
  setTintFill, no BitmapMask, filters instead of FX, and DynamicTexture.render()), Vitest 5, ESLint 10.
- **React** owns the shell: boot and insert-tape screen, main menu, chapter select, settings,
  credits, pause, dialogue box and choices, mission cards, subtitles, toasts, stats screen, touch controls.
- **Phaser** owns the game: one scene per chapter, plus an Attract scene for the menu background
  and a Screening scene.
- **Bridge**: a tiny observable store (`useSyncExternalStore`) plus a promise API
  (`ui.say()`, `ui.choose()`, `ui.missionCard()`), so chapter scripts are linear `async` code.
  Scene shutdown aborts pending promises (no orphaned awaits).
- **Art**: all textures are drawn procedurally with Canvas 2D at boot or per chapter (cut-out rig
  parts, props, backgrounds) → `textures.addCanvas`. No external images.
- **Animation**: bone rig (Container + parts) with named poses, pose tweening and procedural cycles
  (idle breathe, walk, run, skate, dance).
- **Audio**: Web Audio synth engine — a look-ahead step sequencer with instruments (drums, bass,
  leads, organ, piano, strings, brass) for all music, plus synthesized SFX. Voice-over is
  pre-rendered MP3 (Kokoro-82M, Apache-2.0 synthetic voices, not clones of real people),
  lazy-loaded per chapter, ducking music. Context is resumed on the first user gesture (Chrome
  autoplay policy).
- **Saves**: `localStorage` (`tlm.save.v1`, `tlm.settings.v1`) wrapped in try/catch; checkpoint =
  (chapter, beat).
- **Input**: keyboard, mouse, Gamepad API, and touch (virtual stick + buttons) through one input facade.
- **Scaling**: 1280×720 logical, `Scale.FIT` inside a 16:9 CSS stage; overlay UI uses container
  query units so it scales with the stage; `touch-action: none` and `overscroll-behavior: none`.
- **Deployment**: Vite static build → Vercel (framework `vite`, `dist/`), SPA rewrite, immutable
  cache headers for hashed `/assets/*`, and a shorter cache for `/voice/*`.

Directory layout:
```
src/
  components/      React shell UI
  state/           store, save, settings, stats, achievements
  audio/           engine, synth, music (songs), sfx, voice
  game/
    art/           palette, drawing helpers, characters, props, backgrounds
    entities/      Rig, Actor, Scarn, Goon, Goldenface, Samuel, NPC
    systems/       Input, Script (async cutscene), Effects (VHS, freeze, flare), Combat, Stealth, Camera
    minigames/     coin, mop, stick, targets, obstacle, reaction, race, tape, rhythm, hockey, supershot, situp, hack
    scenes/        Boot, Attract, Screening, Ch01…Ch11
  data/            dialogue (with voice ids), chapters, achievements
tools/voice/       offline TTS generation scripts
public/voice/      generated MP3 voice lines
```

## 10. QA Plan (critical paths)

1. Boot → insert tape → menu → PLAY MOVIE → Ch1 fully completable with keyboard+mouse.
2. Every chapter completes from its first checkpoint; every beat restarts cleanly from pause.
3. Scene lifecycle: repeated restart/quit-to-menu/chapter-select does not leak listeners, tweens,
   audio nodes or pending promises (watch the console).
4. Minigames resolve on both success and failure paths; there is no fail-state soft lock (retry or assist).
5. Audio puzzle solvable with audio muted (visual waveform letters).
6. Rhythm timing is stable across frame drops (audio clock); latency offset applies.
7. Hockey: possession, steal, check, shot, goal detection, bomb-puck phase, super shot.
8. Save/continue, chapter unlocks, completion unlocks all, settings persistence, reset save.
9. Pause (Esc/P/Start, visibility change auto-pause), fullscreen toggle, mute.
10. Responsive: 1366×768, 1440×900, 1920×1080, 1024×768 tablet, 390×844 phone (landscape prompt).
11. Lint, typecheck, unit tests (store, save, rhythm judge, rating), production build, preview smoke test.
12. Vercel: SPA fallback, direct reload, asset paths, voice MP3 served, and caching headers.
